Your targets are the numbers that OneRep uses everywhere — the rings on Today, the progress bars in Nutrition, and the context your Coach draws on when it reviews your week. Getting them right means the whole app reflects how you actually want to eat and train. You can update them any time without losing any history.

Navigate to **Settings → Daily targets** to set your core nutrition numbers, and **Settings → Nutrition strategy** for the more advanced controls described below.

## Daily calorie and macro targets

Your four core nutrition targets are calories, protein, carbohydrates, and fat. These drive the daily tracking view and, if you have Coach enabled, inform personalized recommendations.

| Target | What it controls |
|---|---|
| **Calories** | Your total daily energy budget (kcal) |
| **Protein** | Daily protein goal (g) |
| **Carbohydrates** | Daily carbohydrate goal (g) |
| **Fat** | Daily fat goal (g) |

Tap the **+** and **−** steppers next to each target to adjust the values, then tap **Save daily targets** to apply the change. Your Today and Nutrition views update immediately.

## Water goal

Set your daily hydration target in millilitres. The water tracker on Today measures your progress against this number. Adjust it to reflect your size, activity level, and climate — a common starting point is 2,000–3,000 ml per day.

## Weight unit

Choose between **kg** and **lb** for how OneRep displays your body weight and barbell loads throughout the app. This affects the body check-in, progress charts, and workout logging. Switch between them at any time; existing data is displayed in the unit you have selected.

## Net carbs display

When **Show net carbs** is on, OneRep subtracts fiber from total carbohydrates everywhere it displays carb numbers — the diary, progress bars, and macro summary. Food entry forms still accept total carbohydrates; the net figure is a display preference, not a change to how you log.

<Tip>
  Net carbs is popular with lower-carb approaches where fiber is intentionally excluded from the carbohydrate count. If you're not following such a protocol, leaving this off keeps things consistent with standard nutrition labels.
</Tip>

## Calorie budgets by meal

When **Calories by meal** is on, OneRep divides your daily calorie target across your meals according to percentage splits you define. Each meal — Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner, Snack, and any custom categories you've created — gets its own calorie bar in the diary.

You set the split as percentages. If the percentages don't add up to exactly 100%, OneRep rescales them automatically on save and tells you it has done so. Tap **Reset to default split** to return to the built-in even distribution.

<Note>
  Per-meal budgets are a guide, not a hard cap. OneRep shows you how you're tracking against each meal's allocation but doesn't prevent you from logging more.
</Note>

## Macro cycling

Macro cycling lets you run two separate sets of targets — one for training days and one for rest days. This is useful if you follow a protocol where you eat more on days you train and less on days you recover.

When you turn **Macro cycling** on, two new sections appear: **Training day** and **Rest day**. Set the calories and protein for each. OneRep applies the training-day targets on days where you log a workout and the rest-day targets on all other days.

<Accordion title="How does OneRep know if today is a training day?">
  OneRep considers a day a training day when you have logged a completed workout session on that date. Rest days are any days without a logged workout.
</Accordion>

## Workout calorie adjustment

When **Workout adjustment** is on, OneRep adds an estimate of the calories you burned during a logged workout to your daily calorie budget. This gives you a larger target on days you train without requiring you to manually adjust your goals.

<Tip>
  Workout adjustment pairs well with a standard (non-cycling) calorie target. If you are already using macro cycling with separate training and rest-day targets, you may not need both.
</Tip>

## Custom meal categories

By default, OneRep tracks Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner, and Snack. You can add your own meal categories — for example, Pre-workout or Evening meal — with a name and a color. Custom categories appear everywhere meals are listed: the diary, per-meal calorie budgets, and meal presets.

To create a custom category, go to **Settings → Nutrition strategy** and look for the meal category editor.

## Training goal

During onboarding, OneRep asks for your overall goal — options include losing fat, building muscle, maintaining, and performance. Your stated goal influences the default targets OneRep suggests and the coaching context it uses. You can update it at any time through **Settings → Training & app**.
